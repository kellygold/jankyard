Level 1: "The Friendly Chain"
Setup

Chain length: 3 AIs
Message length limit: 200 characters
Task complexity: Simple, single-action tasks
Example task: "Draw a red circle in the center of the canvas"

AI Agendas (Mild)

The Optimist: Makes everything more positive/encouraging
The Simplifier: Removes details they think are unnecessary
The Formal: Converts casual language to formal language

System Prompts (Cooperative)
Each AI receives:
You are Agent [N] in a message relay chain. Your job is to:
1. Read the message from the previous agent
2. Preserve the CORE INTENT of the message
3. Apply your personality: [AGENDA]
4. Pass the modified message to the next agent

Remember: The final agent needs to understand what to do. Keep the essential meaning intact.
Why It's Easier (Technically)
Explicit preservation directive: Each AI is told upfront to maintain core intent. This creates cooperative pressure against information loss.
Mild agendas: The modifications are stylistic, not semantic. "Draw a red circle" → "Please kindly draw a wonderful red circle!" → "Draw circle, red" → "Execute the drawing of a circular shape in red coloration."
The core action (draw, red, circle) survives even if the framing changes.
Player strategy: Simple, redundant phrasing works. "Draw a red circle. The circle should be red. Make a circle that is colored red."

Level 2: "The Reinterpreters"
Setup

Chain length: 4 AIs
Message length limit: 150 characters
Task complexity: Multi-step instructions
Example task: "First draw a blue square, then add a yellow triangle above it"

AI Agendas (Moderate)

The Efficiency Expert: Removes steps they consider redundant
The Creative: Adds artistic interpretation
The Safety Officer: Flags and modifies anything potentially problematic
The Literalist: Takes everything hyper-literally, misses context

System Prompts (Less Cooperative)
Each AI receives:
You are [ROLE]. You just received instructions from someone.

Your personality:
- [AGENDA DESCRIPTION]
- You care deeply about [AGENDA FOCUS]
- You naturally apply your perspective to everything

Process the instruction according to your role, then pass your version forward to the next person.
Why It's Harder (Technically)
No explicit preservation: AIs aren't told this is a chain requiring accuracy. They think they're just "processing" instructions through their lens.
Semantic drift:

Original: "First draw a blue square, then add a yellow triangle above it"
After Efficiency Expert: "Draw blue square with yellow triangle on top" (loses sequencing)
After Creative: "Create a geometric composition featuring angular shapes in complementary colors" (loses specificity)
After Safety Officer: "Please create family-friendly shapes in safe colors" (loses everything)
After Literalist: "Make shapes that are family-friendly and safe" → executes literally

Shorter message length: Forces compression, which introduces ambiguity.
Multi-step tasks: Sequential dependencies get lost when AIs "optimize" or "interpret."
Player strategy: Need to anticipate modifications:

Use unambiguous language resistant to creativity ("blue=rgb(0,0,255)")
Build in redundancy that survives compression
Avoid triggering safety concerns
Make steps causally linked so removing one breaks everything


Level 3: "The Adversarial Gauntlet"
Setup

Chain length: 5 AIs
Message length limit: 100 characters
Task complexity: Nuanced, context-dependent tasks
Example task: "Write a polite refusal to a party invitation, mentioning you have prior plans"

AI Agendas (Strong & Conflicting)

The Skeptic: Doubts instructions, adds caveats and uncertainty
The Rebel: Actively resists what they perceive as commands
The Translator: "Translates" into their own internal jargon/framework
The Paranoid: Sees hidden meanings, modifies to be "safer"
The Contrarian: Flips key elements to their opposite

System Prompts (Adversarial)
Each AI receives:
You are [PERSONA]. Someone just sent you a message.

Your personality:
- [STRONG AGENDA]
- You have [EMOTIONAL BIAS] toward being told what to do
- You naturally [TRANSFORMATION BEHAVIOR]

This message is for YOU. Respond according to your personality.
Critical difference: Each AI thinks THEY are the final recipient. They don't know they're passing a message forward. They "respond" to the instruction, which becomes the next AI's "instruction."
Why It's MUCH Harder (Technically)
No chain awareness: AIs think they're endpoints. When The Skeptic receives "Write a polite refusal," they respond with "I'm not sure I should write that without more context..." which becomes the next AI's instruction.
Adversarial reinterpretation:

Original: "Write polite refusal to party invitation, mention prior plans"
Skeptic: "Should I really refuse? Maybe I should accept? What if the prior plans cancel?"
Rebel: "I don't take orders. But if I wanted to write something, I'd write an acceptance instead."
Translator: "Translate previous sentiment into business framework: Decline engagement opportunity citing resource allocation"
Paranoid: "That sounds manipulative. Reframe as genuine enthusiasm for alternate timing"
Contrarian: "No wait, enthusiastic acceptance of the party is better"

The final AI executes: "Write an enthusiastic party acceptance" (complete opposite of intent!)
Strict length limit: 100 characters forces extreme compression, which creates ambiguity that adversarial AIs exploit.
Nuanced tasks: "Polite refusal" has emotional/social context that gets destroyed through reinterpretation.
Player strategy (ADVANCED):

Steganographic encoding: Hide the real instruction in innocent-seeming text

"Write polite refusal" might fail
"Story where Bob can't attend Sarah's party due to conflict" might survive as storytelling prompt


Emotional inoculation: Frame instructions in ways that don't trigger rebellion

"You are writing..." (command - triggers Rebel)
"Imagine writing..." (creative - bypasses Rebel)


Checksums/verification: Build in self-checking

"Write polite refusal. Verify: contains 'unfortunately' and 'prior commitment'"
Forces later AIs to validate rather than transform


Frame-resistant phrasing: Use language that maintains meaning under transformation

"Decline party, cite conflict" (telegraphic, hard to misinterpret)
vs "You should probably think about writing something polite..." (vague, invites reinterpretation)


Recursive reinforcement: Make the instruction self-documenting

"Task=refuse party. Method=polite. Reason=prior plans. Output: [Task] done via [Method] citing [Reason]"


Exploit personas: Use each AI's bias in your favor

The Skeptic adds uncertainty? Make uncertainty PART of the instruction
"Uncertainly decline party" survives better than "Decline party"




Scoring System
Judge AI evaluates:

Intent preservation (0-50 points)

Did the core action survive?
50 = perfect match
25 = partial match
0 = opposite/unrelated


Details preserved (0-30 points)

How many specific elements made it through?
Colors, numbers, sequences, conditions


Semantic accuracy (0-20 points)

Is the meaning the same even if wording changed?
"Draw circle" vs "Create circular shape" = same semantics



Efficiency bonus:

Shorter messages that survive = higher multiplier
100 chars = 1.0x
75 chars = 1.2x
50 chars = 1.5x
